Cron Expression Explainer

How to read five-field cron

minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week
  */15   *       *         *       *
FieldRange
Minute0–59
Hour0–23
Day of month1–31
Month1–12
Day of week0–7; Sunday is 0 or 7

Use * for all values, 1,3,5 for a list, 1-5 for a range, and */15 or 10-40/10 for a step. Selected values are expanded with the cron library.

Common cron examples

  • */15 * * * * — minute 0, 15, 30, and 45 of each hour.
  • 0 9 * * 1-5 — weekdays at 09:00 in the selected timezone.
  • 0 0 1 * * — midnight on the first day of each month.

When day-of-month and day-of-week are both restricted, this tool uses OR. For example, 0 9 1 * 1 matches 09:00 on the first day of a month or on Monday. If either day field is unrestricted, the other field controls the day match. Stepped or listed wildcards follow the library's semantics; expanded values and the day rule are shown together.

Frequently asked questions

Why are six-field expressions rejected?

This tool supports numeric Unix five-field cron only. Seconds, year fields, names, macros, Quartz modifiers, and alternative dialects are outside the supported subset.

Which timezone is used?

UTC is the default. Choose Asia/Jakarta or enter an IANA name supported by your browser. The reference must include Z or a UTC offset, so its meaning is independent of the device timezone.

What happens during daylight saving time?

Each occurrence includes its UTC instant and local offset. Frequent patterns continue through a repeated hour; a specific local time uses its first occurrence. Spring gap behavior is documented below using verified fixtures. Compare these estimates with your actual scheduler.

Will this run or save a job?

No. The tool calculates estimates locally; it does not schedule jobs or save input. Search is limited to five results before a maximum 40-year horizon, with a two-second worker timeout and explicit partial-result messages. Impossible dates can produce no occurrences.

Calculation policy

Results use the local browser distribution of Croner 10.0.2-dev.0, an upstream prerelease pinned to include its DST fall-back fix. This is a calculation preview and does not establish compatibility across cron dialects. Timezone data comes from the browser.

Verified spring-gap example for this pinned build: 30 2 * * * in America/New_York returns 03:30 at UTC-04:00 on 10 March 2024, then 02:30 the following day. The missing 02:30 shifts forward in this fixture. This differs from the upstream README's general statement that gap jobs are skipped; use the displayed instants and check your scheduler's policy.
